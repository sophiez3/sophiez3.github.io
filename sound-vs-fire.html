Sound vs. Fire — Acoustic Flame Extinction Research

Experimental and theoretical investigation for the United States Invitational Young Physicists Tournament (USIYPT) analyzing the acoustic parameters and flame dynamics governing acoustic flame suppression.

Methodology & Key Findings

  • Damped Harmonic Oscillator Model: Modeled the convective flame body as a damped harmonic oscillator, deriving its theoretical undamped and damped natural frequencies via high-speed video tracking and logarithmic decrement analysis (~57 Hz)[cite: 39, 40, 43, 44, 46].
  • Acoustic Resonance & Extinction Window: Demonstrated via Schlieren imaging and function-generator frequency sweeps that acoustic pressure waves displace oxygen away from the combustion zone[cite: 32, 34, 35], extinguishing single-wick flames fastest within a resonant band of 55–65 Hz (peaking at 0.43 s at 60 Hz)[cite: 37].
  • Intensity & Pressure Amplitude Effects: Measured extinction response across sound intensities at 55 Hz and 60 Hz, identifying an optimal window around 100–102 dB where pressure amplitude maximizes oxygen displacement without excessive air replenishment[cite: 47, 49, 50].
  • Flame Geometry Scaling: Compared single-wick and dual-wick flames at 100 dB, establishing that larger flames require significantly longer exposure times (10.19 s at 60 Hz) and extinguish within a narrower frequency range (60–65 Hz)[cite: 51].
